The **Vimshottari dasha** is the most widely used planetary timeline in Jyotish: a
**120-year cycle** divided among the nine grahas, beginning from the lord of the
nakshatra the Moon occupied at birth. The sequence is fixed — Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon,
Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury — with periods of **7, 20, 6, 10, 7, 18, 16, 19
and 17 years** respectively.

## How the timeline starts

The Moon's exact position inside its janma nakshatra determines the **balance** of
that nakshatra lord's period at birth. If the Moon had traversed half of Rohini
(lord Moon), the birth begins mid-way through a 10-year Moon dasha. That residual
period runs first, and the remaining periods follow the fixed sequence for a
hundred years and more — every graha's turn returns on schedule.

## Mahadasha and antardasha

Each **mahadasha** (major period) subdivides proportionally into nine
**antardashas** (sub-periods), each further into pratyantardashas. Within a single
Jupiter mahadasha, for example, you experience Jupiter–Jupiter, Jupiter–Saturn,
Jupiter–Mercury … in fixed order and length. The kundli page prints your full
mahadasha table with end dates, computed from the Moon's longitude.

## Reading a period

A graha's period behaves like the graha itself: its natural significations (Venus —
relationship and comfort; Saturn — discipline and delay; Jupiter — learning and
expansion), **multiplied by its role in your chart** — the houses and signs it
owns, where it sits, and how strong it is. The same Venus dasha reads very
differently for a Libra Lagna (Venus as Lagna lord) than for a Sagittarius Lagna.

## What dasha is not

It is not fate on rails: dasha frames _emphasis_. Classical practice always reads a
period against the whole chart and against the ongoing transits — Saturn's Sade Sati
([check yours](/calculators/sade-sati)) or Jupiter's current gochar
([transits tool](/calculators/transits)) — before drawing conclusions.
