Jyotish works with **nine grahas (Navagraha)**: the seven visible bodies — Surya (Sun),
Chandra (Moon), Mangala (Mars), Budha (Mercury), Guru (Jupiter), Shukra (Venus), Shani
(Saturn) — plus the two lunar nodes, Rahu and Ketu. The nodes are not physical bodies but
the mathematical intersection points of the Moon's orbit with the ecliptic; classical
Jyotish treats them as full grahas because their effect on a chart is unmistakable.
The outer planets (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto) are not part of the classical scheme.

## What each graha signifies

- **Surya (Sun)** — soul, father, authority, vitality, government. Rules Leo.
- **Chandra (Moon)** — mind, mother, emotions, the everyday fortune of the felt life.
  Rules Cancer. The dasha timeline begins from the Moon's nakshatra.
- **Mangala (Mars)** — energy, courage, siblings, property, conflict when afflicted.
  Rules Aries and Scorpio. Its 1/2/4/7/8/12-house placement is the **Manglik** check
  ([Mangal Dosha tool](/calculators/mangal-dosha)).
- **Budha (Mercury)** — speech, analysis, commerce, learning. Rules Gemini and Virgo.
- **Guru (Jupiter)** — wisdom, dharma, children, expansion, protection. Rules
  Sagittarius and Pisces; exalted in Cancer.
- **Shukra (Venus)** — love, beauty, art, comfort, partnership. Rules Taurus and Libra;
  exalted in Pisces.
- **Shani (Saturn)** — discipline, delay, longevity, karma rendered slowly and fairly.
  Rules Capricorn and Aquarius. Its transit around the Moon sign is **Sade Sati**
  ([Sade Sati tool](/calculators/sade-sati)).
- **Rahu (north node)** — hunger, foreign things, technology, sudden rise; amplifies
  whatever it touches. Its hemming of all grahas produces **Kaal Sarp Yoga**
  ([Kaal Sarp tool](/calculators/kaal-sarp)).
- **Ketu (south node)** — detachment, moksha, past-life skill, sudden insight and
  dissolution.

## Natural friendship

The grahas have standing friendships and enmities that colour every reading: Sun,
Moon, Mars and Jupiter form the "gentle lord" camp; Mercury, Venus and Saturn the
"subtle lord" camp. Mercury treats none as a true enemy; the Moon has none at all —
in the Graha-Maitri koota of [matching](/matching), a Moon-lord pairing always scores
well.

## Strength is contextual

A graha is strong when it sits in its own or exaltation sign, in a good house, unafflicted
and unburnt by the Sun (combustion). The same graha behaves differently in D1 versus
Navamsha ([divisional charts](/calculators/karakas)). Vyotish computes every graha's
exact sidereal longitude — sign, nakshatra, pada, retrogression — so conclusions rest
on precise positions, not sun-sign approximations.
